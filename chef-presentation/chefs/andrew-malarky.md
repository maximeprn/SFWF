**He is not only a participating chef.** The article covers the **first Siargao Food and Wine
Festival** in 2025, where he butchered a 60-pound tuna and, in the writer's description, spent

to speak to the festival's own purpose, it is him.

## Biography

**Boracay, thirteen years.** He defined **Dos Mestizos**, Boracay's Spanish-Filipino landmark —
paella, sangria, guitars. On busy nights he didn't lead from the pass; he walked the dining room
with his sleeves rolled up and treated guests like family. His own summary: *"Boracay gave me
foundation — heritage cuisine, hospitality, and discipline. But a chef's journey is about growth.
You have to keep learning."*

**Toronto.** At **Cluny Bistro** in the Distillery District, 500 covers on a weekend, his
chef-friend Andrew Wilson offered him any station he wanted. He chose **garde manger** — the cold
line usually handed to teenagers — fifteen years after he had last worked it. *"A chef must be
able to handle every station and lead by example."*

**Siargao, 2019.** After thirteen years in Boracay he wanted the challenge. What decided it was
not the surf: *"I noticed how proud people were of keeping the island clean. Everyone tended
their gardens. I thought — this is where I want to grow."*

**The pandemic taught the method.** *"When imports disappeared, I had to forecast carefully,
respect supply chains, and live off the land. You'll never taste anything fresher than what grows
around you."* That constraint became **GLOCAL** — global technique on local produce — and it is
still the restaurant's word for itself.

**Wild.** He co-runs it in General Luna: open-air, unfussy, *more gathering place than dining
room*, with a menu he describes as a work in progress. On why it is not a Filipino restaurant:
*"We noticed the lack of Filipino representation in tourist hotspots. So while our food isn't
fully Filipino, it champions local ingredients and styles."*

**Two dishes the writer singles out** — worth having, because Wild's own site names none:
- **Octopus carpaccio**, tentacles rolled into a log and sliced crosswise into a mosaic — *an
  elevated nod to kinilaw*.
- **A shrimp patty on a sugarcane skewer**, served with **laing purée**.

**How he teaches.** *"I tell my team, don't just learn the how — understand the why."*

## On the festival, in his words

- *"It wasn't just about attracting visitors. It was about creating a platform where farmers,
  cooks, and locals all feel they're part of Siargao's food story."*
- *"The festival is just the beginning. We want growth that's inclusive and conscious, not just
  capitalist."*
- *"Boracay gave me foundation, but Siargao gives me freedom. It's not where, but when."*

The writer's own line is worth noting too: few chefs can claim to have shaped the food story of
two Philippine islands.

## Notes

- **The spelling is unresolved, and the source contradicts itself.** This article's headline says
  **Malarky**, its photo caption and tags say **Malarkey**. Wild's own site says **Malarky** in
  one place and **Andre Malarky** in another. Three spellings across two sources. Ask him.
- **Jove Schrottman is still undocumented.** He co-runs Wild and is named on their site; no
  biography, no quotes, no photograph identified anywhere. He is the last named festival chef
  with nothing behind him, along with **Chef Jose** at Lyma.
- This article dates the **first festival to August 2025**, which makes 2026 the second.
