# Chef blurbs

One short text per chef, **under 150 words**, for a slide or a programme page. Every claim traces
to a source in `../venues/` or `../chefs/`.

**Each one is built around a single idea, not a career.** A chronological summary — trained here,
worked there, now cooks this — is a CV, and it reads like one. So each blurb picks the fact that
reveals the most and opens on it, then lets everything else hang off that:

- Malarky chooses the humbler position, every time. Open on him taking the cold line.
- Luis keeps opening the same restaurant. Open on a boy sent out to sell paella.
- Marc's kitchen is defined by what it keeps. Open on the jars.
- Jarrod does spectacle. Open with the list, unpunctuated, and let it pile up.
- Morris cooked because otherwise nobody ate. Open there.

## If you edit these

- **Don't add adjectives to make a chef sound better.** Nothing here says talented, acclaimed or
  passionate. The facts are flattering on their own; an adjective in front of one only tells the
  reader you didn't trust it.
- **Don't end on a stunt.** An isolated one-line last paragraph reads as a performance. Every one
  of these lands on something the chef does or believes, not on a flourish.
- **Keep the numbers.** Thirteen, 2020, 44,000, five venues, five values. They carry the weight.
- **Three name checks before print:** whether Morris goes by his full name Catanghal; whether
  his Melbourne collaborator is *Abi* or *Abby* Marquez; and whether Wild's chef is *Malarky* or
  *Malarkey* — three spellings appear across two sources.
- Malarky's blurb replaced a joint Wild one once a published interview turned up. It is now the
  most concrete of the five, and it never says he is talented — it says he took the cold line
  after fifteen years away from it, and lets that land.
