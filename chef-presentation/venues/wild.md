## The catering series

Three weddings in spring 2025, all photographed properly. This is the half of Wild that the
restaurant pages don't show — and for a chef presentation it is the evidence they can cook at
scale.

- **Buffet Dinner at Inara** · 30 Mar 2025 · **100 covers**, buffet. The start of a run of weekend
  wedding caterings. Their own note is about the team: professionalism, efficiency, a seamless
  setup.
- **4-Course Dinner at Punta Dolores** · 24 Apr 2025 · **160 covers**, plated four courses, with
  dishes **newly developed for the event**. The most technically ambitious thing on the site.
- **Family Style Buffet at Sandy Feet** · 25 Apr 2025 · an intimate **30 covers**. Hybrid service —
  buffet for appetisers and desserts, mains in large dishes shared at the table. They tie it
  directly to their communal dining philosophy.

The arc reads 100 → 160 → 30 in under a month, which is a better argument for range than any
single photograph.

## Story material

Better than the photographs, and unusually well told on their own site.

**The timeline**
- **2019** — started on a quiet backroad in General Luna. Small room, short menu, "glocal"
  progressive Filipino cooking.
- **2020–21** — opened into the pandemic. A year later **Typhoon Odette** left the island and
  Wild in ruins. *(The site's own heading reads 2000–2021; the typo is theirs.)*
- **2022** — rebuilt as **the Guild Hall of Wild**, a communal room built around long tables.
- **Today** — Malarky and Schrottman carry it forward. Their line: *no borders, no limits* — raw
  yet refined.

**Two events worth a slide of their own**
- **Tip to Tail** (2 editions, 2.0 on 23 Jan 2025) — whole-tuna butchery in front of guests, then
  a menu using every cut. Belgian guest chefs Glenn and Ceasar Vandenberghe. Sustainability and
  technique as spectacle. All the tuna photography above is from this.
- **Raised in the Wild** (4 Jul 2025) — Wild's own crab farm in **Del Carmen**, the third largest
  mangrove reserve in the Philippines. Soft-shell and mud crabs grown by **Bryan** in his backyard
  farm and caught from the mangroves.

Note the rhyme with Alma: **both opened during the pandemic and both were destroyed by Odette.**
Two of the festival's venues share that spine, and neither tells it as a hardship story.
